# The 3 Bait Question Scripts

Use these verbatim, or adapt the pattern. The goal is the same: a question
**only your family can answer** that a voice clone — no matter how good —
cannot.

---

## Script 1 — The "Family Memory" Bait

*"Okay, before we talk money — quick check. What did we eat at Grandma's
80th birthday?"*

**Why it works:** The answer is a shared memory, never posted online, and
the clone has zero access to it. A real relative answers in one second. A
clone stalls, guesses, or deflects.

**Variations:**
- "What was the name of the place we went for Dad's retirement?"
- "Who caught the fish on the lake trip in 2019?"
- "What song did we dance to at your wedding?"

---

## Script 2 — The "Inside Joke" Bait

*"Hold on — you remember the thing with the ferry? What happened?"*

**Why it works:** Inside jokes are the ultimate private data. They're never
written down, never posted, and completely unpredictable. The clone cannot
even identify what you're referring to.

**Variations:**
- "Remember Uncle Roy's hat? What color was it?"
- "What did we name the raccoon that lived in the shed?"
- "You still owe me from the bet at the cabin. What was it about?"

---

## Script 3 — The "Mutual Context" Bait (for business)

*"Before I touch that wire — which vendor was the one with the invoice
problem last quarter? And what's my extension?"*

**Why it works:** Business scams rely on the caller (the "CEO") being the
one with all the context. Flipping it — requiring the caller to prove
knowledge only the real person has — breaks the script instantly.

**Variations:**
- "What's the name of the project we killed in March?"
- "Which client did we lose the contract to last year?"
- "What code word did we agree on for urgent requests?"

---

## The 3 Rules of Bait Questions

1. **Ask FIRST, pay later.** Verification happens before any information
   about the emergency is shared — never after.
2. **Never verify with the caller's facts.** If the caller says "I'm at St.
   Mary's Hospital," do NOT ask "which hospital are you at?" — they'll
   repeat it. Ask something they *couldn't* know.
3. **One wrong answer = fail.** No second chances, no "oh I meant the other
   one." The moment the answer is wrong, the call is a scam. Hang up.

---

## The 30-Second Drill

Practice this exchange until it's automatic:

> **Caller:** "Mom, it's me, I'm in trouble, I need money!"
> **You:** "What's the word?"
> **Caller:** "What? Mom, there's no time!"
> **You:** "What did we eat at Grandma's 80th?"
> **Caller:** "I don't remember, please!"
> **You:** "Then you're not my [son/daughter]. Goodbye."
