## Part 1 — The Threat Model

### 1.1 How Voice Cloning Works (Plain English)

Voice cloning systems (the kind freely available today) work like this:

1. **Harvest** — the scammer finds 3–60 seconds of clean audio of a target's
   loved one. Sources: social media videos, voicemail greetings, group chats,
   podcast appearances, even recordings from data breaches.
2. **Train** — the audio is fed into a cloning model. Modern models need as
   little as 3 seconds to produce a convincing imitation; 30–60 seconds
   produces a near-perfect clone including tone, pacing, and tics.
3. **Deploy** — the scammer places a call (or WhatsApp/Voice message) with
   spoofed caller ID showing the loved one's real number. The clone speaks
   live, or a pre-recorded panic message is played.
4. **Pressure** — the message creates urgency and isolation: *"I'm in jail /
   hospital / stranded. I need money NOW. Don't tell Mom."*

The critical insight: **the scammer never needed to sound perfect.** They
only need to sound *plausible enough* during a moment of panic, when the
victim's rational mind is offline.

### 1.2 Who Gets Targeted (And Why)

- **Parents & grandparents (55+):** most targeted, most susceptible to
  panic-based appeals, highest average savings. The "grandparent scam" has
  existed for decades; AI voice cloning removes its biggest weakness (the
  voice didn't sound right).
- **Small business owners & finance staff:** the "CEO fraud" variant — a
  cloned voice of the boss calls the bookkeeper demanding an urgent wire.
  Losses average far higher per incident.
- **Anyone with a public voice:** if you've ever been on a podcast, a
  webinar, a local news clip, or a viral TikTok — you have a cloneable voice
  footprint. If your *parents* have one, they're targets.

### 1.3 The Economics of the Scam

- Average successful voice-clone fraud: **$11,000+ per incident** (IC3).
- A scammer needs **minutes** of setup and **zero** technical skill (GUI
  tools are sold openly).
- The call costs nothing. The payoff can be a lifetime of savings.
- **One stopped wire pays for this guide hundreds of times over.**

### 1.4 What Voice Clones CAN and CANNOT Do

| CAN do | CANNOT do |
|---|---|
| Mimic tone, accent, pacing, tics | Know your family's private shared secrets |
| Speak live or pre-recorded | Pass a live video verification |
| Spoof caller ID | Correct a deliberately wrong statement |
| Sound panicked/urgent | Know the answer to a bait question |
| Reference public facts | Know who visited last weekend |

The Protocol is built entirely on the right-hand column.

## Part 2 — The Protocol: Establish → Verify → Challenge → Escalate

### 2.1 The Four Steps At A Glance

- **E — ESTABLISH** the shared secret *before* you need it (safe word +
  bait questions + hang-up script), with every family member and key
  colleague.
- **V — VERIFY** any emotional or money request using the secret. Never
  verify with information the caller *offers* you — verify with information
  *only your family knows*.
- **C — CHALLENGE** anything that fails verification: a wrong safe word, a
  missed bait question, refusal to do video, pressure to act instantly.
- **E — ESCALATE** to the human chain: hang up, call the real person on
  their *known* number, notify the family group, and if money moved — the
  72-hour recovery playbook.

### 2.2 Step 1: ESTABLISH (Tonight, 20 minutes)

**The Safe Word.** Choose a word or phrase that:
- Is **never** posted online, in texts, or in voicemail greetings.
- Is memorable but unpredictable (not pet names or birthdays — those are
  public).
- Has a **backup** for the "I forgot" case: a second word, or a physical
  object ("the blue mug in the kitchen").

Example patterns that work: *"pineapple umbrella,"* *"Uncle Roy's hat,"*
*"the word is sideways."* Random two-word combinations beat single words.

**The Bait Questions.** Choose 3 questions only family would know:
1. *"What did we eat at Grandma's 80th?"*
2. *"What color was my first car?"*
3. *"Who threw up on the ferry in 2019?"*

**The Hang-Up Script.** Agree that ANY call matching the scam pattern gets
this response, no exceptions:

> *"I need to verify this. I'm hanging up and calling you back on your
> number. If this is really you, you'll answer. If it's not — goodbye."*

**The Family Group.** Create a private group chat (WhatsApp/Signal/iMessage)
named something innocuous like "Weekend Plans." Rule: **any money request by
voice triggers a group post within 60 seconds.** The scammer can't be in the
group.

**The Business Version.** For owners/accounting staff: agree that **no
verbal instruction can move money**. Wires require (a) a written request via
the approved channel, AND (b) a call-back to the requester's *known* number.
Two-factor for cash. No exceptions, ever.

### 2.3 Step 2: VERIFY (Every Emotional Call)

When a call or message triggers ANY of these:
- A loved one in "trouble" (jail, hospital, stranded, lawyer, bail)
- Urgency + secrecy ("don't tell anyone")
- A request for money, gift cards, crypto, or wire transfer
- Caller ID showing a family number, but the voice "sounds off"

...you run VERIFICATION, in this order:

1. **Safe word:** *"Before we go further — what's the word?"* A real family
   member knows it instantly. A clone freezes, deflects, or invents.
2. **Bait question:** *"Okay, and what did we eat at Grandma's 80th?"* The
   clone cannot know. Even a *wrong* answer is a fail.
3. **Video:** *"Let me see you. FaceTime me right now."* A live clone cannot
   hold a real-time video conversation with your family's private context.
4. **Call-back:** hang up and dial the person's **known** number from your
   contacts — not the number that called you. If the line is busy, the
   family group is your backup verification.

**The Golden Rule: you never verify WITH the caller. You verify the caller
against what YOU already know.**

### 2.4 Step 3: CHALLENGE (The Clone's Kryptonite)

If verification fails, the clone will try to recover. Expect these
tactics — and counter each one:

| Clone tactic | Your challenge |
|---|---|
| *"I'm injured, I can't think straight!"* | *"Then you'll understand why I need the word. What is it?"* |
| *"This is urgent, there's no time!"* | *"There's always time to verify. One word. That's all."* |
| *"Don't you recognize my voice?!"* | *"I recognize that clones exist. What's the word?"* |
| *"I'll get in trouble if you tell anyone!"* | *"The only trouble is if I send money to a stranger. Hanging up now."* |
| *"I'm calling from a friend's phone."* | *"Great — call me from your number, or FaceTime me. I'll wait."* |
| *"Mom would be so disappointed..."* | *"Mom would be proud of me for verifying. What's the word?"* |

The pattern: **every challenge redirects back to the secret.** You never
argue, you never apologize, you never "help" a stranger prove they're family.
The burden of proof is always on the caller.

### 2.5 Step 4: ESCALATE (When It's Real, Or When You're Unsure)

- **Hang up.** Always. Even if you're 90% sure it's real — the 10% is what
  the Protocol protects.
- **Call the real person** on their known number. Busy? Try the spouse, the
  office, the other parent.
- **Post in the family group** within 60 seconds: *"Possible scam call
  using [name]'s voice. Verify before any money moves."* This turns one
  person's uncertainty into the family's collective memory.
- **If money already moved:** STOP. Do not "trust" the caller's instructions
  to "fix" it (that's a second scam). Follow the 72-Hour Recovery Playbook
  (Part 4) immediately.

## Part 3 — The 9 Red Flags (Printable Checklist)

A call is **AI-generated or fraudulent** if ANY of these appear:

1. **The panic hook** — instant emergency: jail, hospital, lawyer, stranded.
2. **Secrecy pressure** — "don't tell anyone," "don't call the office."
3. **Money in a hurry** — wire, gift cards, crypto, cash app, "right now."
4. **Wrong safe word** — or refusal/deflection when asked.
5. **Missed bait question** — vague answer, changes the subject.
6. **No video** — excuses when asked to FaceTime.
7. **Spoofed caller ID** — number looks right but call-back fails.
8. **"Sound is bad"** — classic clone cover story for audio glitches.
9. **The ask is off-pattern** — a request that "never happens" in your
   family's real relationship.

**If 2+ flags are present: HANG UP. VERIFY. CHALLENGE. ESCALATE.**

## Part 4 — The "I Already Sent Money" Recovery Playbook (72-Hour)

If you or a family member already transferred money, **time is everything.**
Most fraud is recoverable within the first hours; almost none is recoverable
after 72.

### Hour 0–1: Freeze Everything

1. **Call your bank/financial institution IMMEDIATELY** — use the number on
   the back of your card, NOT any number the caller provided. Say:
   *"I believe I'm the victim of fraud. I need to freeze the transfer and
   file a claim."*
2. **Wire transfers:** ask to recall/reverse the wire. Banks have a recall
   window; every minute counts. Fedwire: your bank files a recall request
   through the receiving bank.
3. **Gift cards:** call the issuer (e.g., Visa/Mastercard gift card support)
   with the card numbers. Some can freeze or refund unredeemed balances.
4. **Crypto:** transfers are irreversible, but report to the exchange used
   (e.g., Coinbase) and to chain-analysis partners — some exchanges can flag
   the receiving wallet.
5. **Zelle/CashApp/Venmo:** file a dispute in-app immediately; contact
   support and escalate to fraud.

### Hour 1–24: Document and Report

6. **Preserve everything:** call logs, texts, the cloned voicemail/audio,
   transaction receipts, the exact number called from. Do NOT delete — this
   is evidence.
7. **Report to the FBI IC3** at ic3.gov — file a complaint with all details.
   This is required for most bank recovery processes and helps stop the next
   victim.
8. **Report to the FTC** at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
9. **Report to your state/local police** — get a case number; banks often
   require it.
10. **Notify your bank's fraud department** in writing (email) so there's a
    paper trail with timestamps.

### Day 1–3: Escalate and Protect

11. **Escalate at the bank:** if the first agent says no, ask for the fraud
    department, then the ombudsman/compliance. Be polite, persistent, and
    reference your IC3 complaint number.
12. **Check other accounts:** change passwords, enable 2FA, freeze credit
    (Equifax/Experian/TransUnion), alert the family group so the scammer
    can't re-target with the same script.
13. **Talk to a human:** the emotional toll is real. This was a crime
    against you, not a personal failure. Tell someone you trust.

### The Truth About Recovery

- **Bank/wire fraud reported within hours: good recovery odds.**
- **Gift cards/crypto: low odds, report anyway.**
- **The scammer will likely call again** — often pretending to be a
  "recovery agent" who can get your money back for a fee. That is a
  SECOND scam. Report, don't re-engage.

## Part 5 — The Parent & Grandparent Conversation Guide

The most-targeted generation is also the hardest to reach. Fear-based
warnings make them anxious and defensive. This is how you actually protect
them.

### 5.1 Frame It As Love, Not Fear

Don't lead with statistics. Lead with the relationship:

> *"Mom, I want us to have a code word — just for emergencies. If I ever
> call you in real trouble, I'll say it, and you'll know it's really me.
> And if someone calls claiming to be me and doesn't know the word, you'll
> know it's a fake and you can hang up safely. It's my job to look out for
> you, and this is how I do it."*

### 5.2 Make It Their Idea (The Two-Question Method)

1. *"Have you heard about those calls where they copy a voice?"* — let them
   tell you what they know; correct gently.
2. *"Would it feel safer if we had a way to know it's really me every
   time?"* — now it's their decision, not your lecture.

### 5.3 Keep It Simple: Three Rules Only

1. **The word:** "We have one word. If I ever call in trouble, I'll say it.
   Anyone who can't say it is not me."
2. **The call-back:** "If someone asks for money, hang up and call me back
   on MY number."
3. **The group:** "You're in the family group. If you're ever unsure, post
   there — we'll answer in minutes."

### 5.4 Rehearse Without Scaring

Run a **practice call** at a normal moment: *"Okay Mom, pretend I'm a
scammer — what do you ask me?"* Praise the correct behavior. Repetition
builds the reflex so the panic moment is handled by habit, not judgment.

### 5.5 What NOT To Do

- Don't show them scary videos of cloned voices (creates anxiety without
  skills).
- Don't shame them for almost falling for it — shame kills the reporting
  reflex, which is the single most valuable thing they can do.
- Don't make the safe word a family joke posted on Facebook — public
  defeats the purpose.

## Part 6 — The Business Edition (CEO Fraud Defense)

Businesses lose more per incident than families — and the clone doesn't even
need to be of a relative. It needs to be of the **boss**.

### 6.1 The Attack Pattern

1. Scammer clones the CEO/CFO voice from earnings calls, YouTube, podcasts.
2. Calls the bookkeeper/AP clerk with spoofed caller ID.
3. Script: urgent acquisition / vendor dispute / tax issue — wire $X now.
4. Adds secrecy: *"This is confidential, don't tell anyone."*

### 6.2 The Business Protocol

- **No verbal wire, ever.** Written request via approved channel (email/ERP)
  + call-back to a *known* number + second approver for anything above a
  set threshold.
- **The 30-second rule:** any request for urgent funds triggers a mandatory
  in-person or video confirmation with the requester.
- **Named verification phrase** for executives (the corporate safe word).
- **Train AP/finance staff annually** with a live drill: send a fake cloned
  request and see who processes it. Reward the ones who refuse.
- **Insure:** wire-fraud/cyber policies exist and are cheap relative to the
  exposure. Ask your broker about "social engineering fraud" coverage.

### 6.3 The 5-Second Executive Habit

Every executive should say this in any training or all-hands:

> *"I will never ask you to wire money by voice or by urgent text. If you
> ever receive such a request claiming to be from me — it is a scam. Hang
> up, and confirm with me in person."*

## Part 8 — Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if a real family member forgets the safe word?**
A: That's what the backup is for (second word or physical object). If they
fail both, they call back on their known number — a scammer can't do that.

**Q: What if the scammer knows my voice is cloned? They could guess.**
A: The safe word is never spoken, posted, or texted. It exists only in
family memory. There is nothing to guess.

**Q: Can't they clone the safe word from a voicemail?**
A: Only if you record it — which is why the rule is: never say the word into
voicemail, texts, or social audio. Keep it spoken-only, in person.

**Q: What about video calls — can they deepfake those too?**
A: Real-time video deepfakes exist but are expensive and fragile; the
combination of video + safe word + bait questions is beyond the economics of
the average scam. Video alone isn't enough — always pair it with the secret.
